Work Experience & Personal Statement

Work experience matters less for how much you did than for what you learned from it. The personal statement is where you show that reflection.

Work experience

  • Dental practice shadowing: e.g. Birmingham usually expects at least 3 days in an NHS or mixed practice.
  • Caring roles: care homes, hospices, hospital volunteering. Regular commitment over months is valued.
  • No placement? Schools say you won't be disadvantaged, but you must still understand the job. Use online resources and talk to dental professionals.
  • Keep a reflective diary after every session, without patient-identifiable details.

NHS Constitution values

Working together for patients · Respect and dignity · Commitment to quality of care · Compassion · Improving lives · Everyone counts.

The personal statement (2026 entry onwards)

QuestionFor dentistry, cover
1. Why do you want to study this course or subject?Specific motivation from experience; why dentistry, not another health career
2. How have your qualifications and studies helped you prepare?Chemistry and Biology links, practical skills, independent reading, EPQ
3. What else have you done outside education, and why is it useful?Work experience and volunteering (reflected), dexterity, teamwork, resilience

4,000 characters including spaces in total; at least 350 per answer. One statement goes to all your choices (source).

Tips

  • One reflected example beats a list of placements.
  • Avoid clichés; show what you noticed and how it changed your view.
  • Don't include your UCAT score; it's sent separately.
  • Start drafting in summer 2027 and get teacher feedback.

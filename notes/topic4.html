Dental Caries & Fluoride

Caries happens when plaque bacteria turn sugar into acid faster than saliva can repair the enamel. How often sugar is eaten matters more than how much.

The four things caries needs

  1. A tooth surface
  2. Plaque bacteria: Streptococcus mutans, then lactobacilli
  3. Fermentable sugar, especially sucrose
  4. Time

How a cavity develops

  1. White spot lesion: chalky, still reversible
  2. Enamel cavity: surface breaks down
  3. Dentine: decay spreads faster along softer dentine
  4. Pulp: pulpitis, pain, infection, abscess

Critical pH

TissueStarts to dissolve below
Enamel (hydroxyapatite)~pH 5.5
Fluorapatite~pH 4.5
Root dentine~pH 6.2–6.7

Fluoride works three ways

  1. Forms fluorapatite, which resists acid
  2. Speeds remineralisation
  3. Inhibits bacterial enzymes (enolase)
Ca₁₀(PO₄)₆(OH)₂ + 2F⁻ → Ca₁₀(PO₄)₆F₂ + 2OH⁻

Prevention (UK advice)

  • Adult toothpaste 1,350–1,500 ppm fluoride, twice daily; spit, don't rinse
  • Keep sugar to mealtimes
  • Fluoride varnish and fissure sealants
  • Main guidance: Delivering Better Oral Health

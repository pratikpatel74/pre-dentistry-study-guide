Interviews & MMIs

Interviews test whether you understand dentistry, communicate well and think ethically. They aren't a science exam. Practice with feedback makes a big difference.

Formats

  • MMI (multiple mini-interviews): a circuit of short stations (often 5–10 minutes each), each scored separately. Used by about 8 UK dental schools.
  • Panel: one longer interview with two or three interviewers. Used by about 4 schools. Formats change, so check each year.

Frameworks

  • STARR for experiences: Situation, Task, Action, Result, Reflection.
  • Four pillars for ethics: autonomy, beneficence, non-maleficence, justice. See Topic 8 for consent and capacity.
  • SPIKES for breaking bad news: Setting, Perception, Invitation, Knowledge, Empathy, Summary.
  • For dilemmas: patient safety first, gather facts, don't act alone, escalate appropriately.

NHS dentistry topics to know (England, 2026)

  • Access: many people struggle to find an NHS dentist.
  • Contract changes from April 2026: practices must provide urgent care (within 24 hours for urgent cases, a week for non-urgent); new care pathways for patients with high needs. The UDA system remains; wider reform is still promised (summary).
  • Prevention: water fluoridation, the Soft Drinks Industry Levy (2018), Delivering Better Oral Health.
  • Children's decay: tooth extraction is one of the most common reasons young children are admitted to hospital.
  • Workforce and skill mix: therapists and hygienists doing more; recruitment to NHS practice.

Sample questions to practise

  1. Why dentistry, and why not medicine or dental therapy?
  2. Tell us about your work experience. What did you learn?
  3. What makes a good dentist? Give an example from your experience.
  4. How would you deal with a patient who is very anxious?
  5. A friend admits cheating in an exam. What would you do?
  6. What are the biggest challenges facing NHS dentistry?

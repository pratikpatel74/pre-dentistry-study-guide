Head & Neck Anatomy Essentials

The trigeminal nerve (CN V) is the most important structure for a dentist: it carries sensation from the teeth and is the nerve you numb with local anaesthetic.

Trigeminal nerve: three branches

BranchSuppliesDental relevance
V1 OphthalmicForehead, upper eyelid, noseRarely relevant
V2 MaxillaryUpper teeth, upper lip, palate, cheekUpper teeth usually numbed by infiltration
V3 MandibularLower teeth, lower lip, tongue sensation, chin; chewing musclesLower molars usually need an inferior alveolar nerve block

The facial nerve (CN VII) moves the face. A misplaced block can cause a temporary one-sided droop.

Muscles of mastication (all V3)

  • Masseter: closes; strongest
  • Temporalis: closes and retracts
  • Medial pterygoid: closes
  • Lateral pterygoid: opens, protrudes, moves side to side

TMJ

Mandibular condyle against the temporal bone, with a fibrous disc between. It hinges and glides.

Salivary glands

The UCAT

The UCAT is a 2-hour computer test of aptitude, not knowledge. You sit it once, in the summer before you apply (summer 2027 for 2028 entry). Timing is the hardest part.

The four subtests (standard timings)

SubtestQuestionsTimeTestsScored
Verbal Reasoning4422 minReading and evaluating passages300–900
Decision Making3537 minLogic and judgements from information300–900
Quantitative Reasoning3626 minNumbers in charts and tables300–900
Situational Judgement6926 minAppropriate behaviour in clinical scenariosBand 1–4

Plus 6½ minutes of instructions: 117½ minutes in total. Cognitive total: 900–2700. Abstract Reasoning was removed from 2025. Source: UCAT.

How schools use it

  • Each school uses it differently: cut-off scores, points in a scoring system, or ranking for interview.
  • A Band 4 SJT can rule you out at some schools.
  • Results arrive straight after the test and go to universities automatically.

How to prepare

  1. Register when it opens (spring 2027) and book an early summer slot.
  2. Start with the official practice tests on the UCAT website.
  3. Practise each subtest under strict timing, then full mock tests.
  4. Learn the on-screen calculator and keyboard shortcuts.
  5. For SJT, think like a professional: patient safety, honesty, teamwork.

Extra time (UCATSEN, +25%) is available for eligible candidates; apply before booking.

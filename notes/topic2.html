Tooth Anatomy & Tissues

Every tooth has a crown covered in enamel and a root covered in cementum, built around dentine with living pulp at the centre.

The tissues

TissueMade ofKey facts
Enamel~96% mineral (hydroxyapatite)Hardest substance in the body; no living cells, cannot regrow
Dentine~70% mineral + collagen, waterHas tubules, so exposed dentine is sensitive; made by odontoblasts throughout life
PulpSoft tissue, nerves, vesselsKeeps the tooth alive; pulpitis causes pain
Cementum~45–50% mineralCovers the root; anchors ligament fibres
Periodontal ligamentCollagen fibresHolds the tooth; senses biting pressure
Alveolar boneBoneForms the socket; shrinks after tooth loss
GingivaMucosaSeals around the neck of the tooth

Naming surfaces

  • Mesial (towards the midline) and distal (away from it)
  • Buccal (towards the cheek, back teeth) and labial (towards the lips, front teeth)
  • Lingual (towards the tongue, lower teeth) and palatal (towards the palate, upper teeth)
  • Occlusal (biting surface, back teeth) and incisal (biting edge, front teeth)

Landmarks

  • CEJ: where enamel meets cementum. Apical foramen: where nerves and vessels enter. Furcation: where roots divide.

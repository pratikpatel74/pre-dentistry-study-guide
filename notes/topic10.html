Dental Materials

You'll handle these materials from your first phantom-head sessions. Know what each is made of, how it sets and when it's chosen.

Amalgam: the rules now

  • Great Britain: since 2018 not normally used for baby teeth, under-15s, or pregnant or breastfeeding women, unless strictly necessary. Not banned.
  • EU: banned for most uses from 1 January 2025. Northern Ireland has a derogation to 2034.
  • Worldwide: in November 2025 the Minamata Convention agreed to end manufacture, import and export by 2034.

Impressions and models

  • Alginate (irreversible hydrocolloid): quick and cheap, for study models; pour promptly.
  • Silicones / elastomers: accurate and stable, for crowns and bridges.
  • Gypsum (plaster, dental stone): poured into impressions to make models.

Crowns

  • Zirconia: very strong, tooth-coloured. Porcelain / glass ceramics: most natural-looking. PFM: porcelain fused to metal. Gold: strong, kind to opposing teeth. Stainless steel: preformed crowns for children's back teeth.
